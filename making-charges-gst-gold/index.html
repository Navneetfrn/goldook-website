Making charges and GST on gold

A jewellery bill has three parts. Knowing them helps you compare shops and avoid paying for things twice.

The three parts of a jewellery bill

  1. Gold value = gold weight × the day's rate for that purity (for example 22K).
  2. Making charges for the craftsmanship: either a fixed amount per gram or a percentage of the gold value.
  3. GST at 3% on the gold value plus the making charges.

A worked example

Example figures only; use the day's actual rate and your jeweller's actual charges.

Gold: 10 g of 22K at ₹10,000 per gram₹1,00,000
Making charges: 12% of the gold value₹12,000
Subtotal₹1,12,000
GST: 3% of ₹1,12,000₹3,360
Total to pay₹1,15,360

Checklist before you pay

  • The bill shows the gold weight, purity and rate separately from making charges and GST.
  • Stones are weighed and priced separately, not at the gold rate.
  • The piece carries a BIS hallmark with a HUID, and the HUID is on the bill. How to check it.
  • Ask about the exchange and buy-back policy: what will be deducted if you sell or exchange later.
  • Keep the original bill. You need it to prove ownership, for insurance and for a gold loan.

Common questions

How are making charges calculated?

Jewellers charge either a fixed amount per gram or a percentage of the gold value. It varies a lot by design and shop, so ask for the figure in writing before you buy.

How much GST is charged on gold jewellery?

GST on gold jewellery is 3%, charged on the gold value plus the making charges. Your bill should show it separately.

Do I get making charges back when I sell?

Usually not. When you sell or exchange jewellery, you are normally paid for the gold content at the day's rate, minus any deductions the buyer applies. Making charges and GST are not returned.

Are stones charged at the gold rate?

They should not be. Check that the weight of stones is listed separately and that you are charged the gold rate only for the gold weight.
