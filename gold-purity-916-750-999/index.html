916, 750 and 999: gold purity explained

Those small numbers stamped on your jewellery tell you how much of it is pure gold, and so what it is worth.

Gold purity in India is written two ways: in carats (out of 24) and as fineness (parts per thousand). They say the same thing. 22 carat means 22 parts gold out of 24, which is 916 parts in 1,000.

How purity changes the price

The gold in a piece is worth its weight × the 24K rate × its purity. So 10 grams of 22K gold contains 9.16 grams of pure gold, and 10 grams of 18K contains 7.5 grams. That is why the rate for 22K is about 91.6% of the 24K rate. See today's 24K, 22K and 18K rates.

Where to find the purity mark

Gold jewellery sold in India carries a BIS hallmark: the BIS logo, the purity grade (such as 22K916) and a unique 6-character HUID code. It is usually stamped on the clasp or inside a ring or bangle. Here is how to check a HUID.

Common questions

What does 916 mean on gold?

916 means the gold is 91.6% pure, which is 22 carat. It comes from 22 ÷ 24 = 0.916. Most Indian jewellery is 22K (916).

Is 24K gold used for jewellery?

Rarely. 24K (999) is very soft, so it is used mainly for coins and bars. Jewellery is usually 22K, or 18K when it holds stones.

Is 18K gold real gold?

Yes. 18K (750) is 75% pure gold mixed with other metals to make it stronger. It is common in diamond and studded jewellery and is priced lower per gram than 22K.

How do I check purity without a lab?

Look for the BIS hallmark: the BIS logo, the purity grade (for example 22K916) and a 6-character HUID. Home tests such as magnets or biting are not reliable.
