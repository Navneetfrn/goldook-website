How to make a Will for your gold in India

Gold is often a family's most valuable possession, and the most argued about. A clear Will settles who gets what.

In India, jewellery often passes through generations without anything written down. When there is no Will, gold is shared under the law of inheritance, which may not match what you wanted, and families can fall out over who was promised which piece. A Will that lists each item prevents that.

5 steps to a Will for your gold

  1. List every piece. For each item write what it is, its weight in grams, its purity (for example 22K / 916), its hallmark HUID number, and where it is kept (home, bank locker). Keep photos and bills together.
  2. Choose who gets each item. Use each person's full name and relationship to you. Add a backup person in case someone dies before you.
  3. Name an executor. A trusted adult who will make sure your wishes are carried out.
  4. Sign in front of two witnesses. Sign (and initial every page) while two adults watch; they then sign too. Choose witnesses who receive nothing under the Will.
  5. Register it and keep it safe. Registration with the Sub-Registrar is optional but makes the Will harder to dispute. Tell your executor where the original is.

Things people often get wrong

  • Locker nominee ≠ owner. A bank locker nominee can collect the contents, but ownership follows your Will.
  • Vague wording. "My jewellery to my daughters" leads to arguments. Name each piece.
  • Old Wills. If you sell, gift or buy gold, update the Will. A new Will should say it cancels earlier ones.
  • Personal law. Muslim law limits what can be left by Will to people who are not heirs (generally up to one-third) unless the heirs agree. Ask a lawyer if this applies to you.

Common questions

Is a handwritten or typed Will valid in India?

Yes, both are valid. Under the Indian Succession Act, 1925, a Will must be in writing, signed by the person making it, and attested by at least two witnesses who saw them sign. Stamp paper is not needed.

Does a Will need to be registered in India?

No, registration is optional. Registering it with the Sub-Registrar keeps a safe official copy and makes it harder to dispute, so many families choose to.

Can a person who gets gold in my Will be a witness?

Avoid it. If a witness or their spouse is a beneficiary, the gift to them can become void, even though the rest of the Will stays valid. Choose two adults who receive nothing under the Will.

Is the bank locker nominee the owner of the gold inside?

Not automatically. A locker nominee can open and collect the contents, but who owns the gold is decided by your Will, or by the law of inheritance if there is no Will. A Will that lists the jewellery avoids family disputes.

Can I change my Will later?

Yes, any time. Make a new Will that says it cancels earlier ones, and sign it with two witnesses again. The last valid Will is the one that counts.
